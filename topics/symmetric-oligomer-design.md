# Symmetric oligomer design

A chapter on the design of protein assemblies in which several copies of one designed chain are arranged under a **point-group symmetry** — cyclic (C*n*) rings, dihedral (D*n*) stacks, and the cubic groups (tetrahedral, octahedral, icosahedral). The interesting thing about symmetric homo-oligomers, from a design standpoint, is that they let one small design decision — a single protomer and a single interface — determine a much larger object. The interesting thing historically is that this problem has been solved three separate times, by three genuinely different mechanisms, in roughly thirty years: symmetry as **geometry** (imposed exactly, by construction), symmetry as an **objective** (rewarded, by optimization against a structure predictor), and symmetry as a **constraint on a sampling process** (enforced at every step of denoising). That progression, and what each step cost and bought, is the argument of this chapter.

## 1. Why symmetry is a design primitive

Symmetric assemblies are not a niche of the natural protein world. Cyclic protein oligomers alone constitute nearly **30% of all deposited PDB structures**, and they participate in almost every biological process ([[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]]). The reason usually offered is energetic: symmetric interfaces can be favourable under evolutionary pressure, and natural proteins accordingly oligomerize into linear, cyclic, dihedral, cubic, planar and spatial symmetries ([[luo-2016-protein-assembly-review]]).

For a designer the appeal is combinatorial. If *n* copies of one chain are arranged under a point group, then designing one protomer and one protomer–protomer interface specifies the entire assembly; the relative orientation of two adjacent subunits together with the complementarity of their contact interface determines the multiplicity of the whole complex ([[luo-2016-protein-assembly-review]]). A designed ring is therefore cheap in design effort relative to its size — the property that makes symmetric oligomers attractive as vaccine platforms, delivery vehicles and catalysts ([[watson-2023-rfdiffusion]]), as building blocks for nanocage assemblies and as substrates for small-molecule binding and catalysis ([[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]]).

The costs are equally structural. An error in the single designed interface is repeated *n* times, so failure modes are amplified rather than averaged out. And the geometry is unforgiving: for fusion-based approaches, a specific arrangement between the symmetry axes of two different oligomeric proteins is required for the design to be accurate at all ([[luo-2016-protein-assembly-review]]). Symmetry makes the design problem smaller and the tolerance for error smaller at the same time.

## 2. The pre-learning era — borrow, parameterize, or dock

Before deep learning there were three ways to obtain a symmetric assembly, and it is worth being precise about them because the later methods are best understood as removing specific constraints that each imposed.

**Borrow the symmetry.** The oldest computational route takes symmetry from nature rather than creating it. Yeates and co-workers introduced **genetic oligomeric fusion**: two natural oligomeric domains — a dimeric M1 matrix protein and a trimeric bromoperoxidase — held in a predetermined orientation by a semirigid helical linker, self-assembling into a **12-subunit tetrahedral cage** when the geometric rule that the 3-fold and 2-fold rotational axes intersect at an appropriate non-orthogonal angle was satisfied; substituting a dimerizing carboxylesterase for the trimeric partner produced filaments instead ([[luo-2016-protein-assembly-review]]). A later refinement of the same design yielded a homogeneous **12-subunit, 16-nm cage with a 5-nm central opening**, resolved crystallographically, and the approach was extended to porous cubic cages — a KDPGal–FkpA fusion with **225 Å outer and 132 Å inner diameter** under octahedral symmetry — and to symmetry-matching fusions producing 1D filaments, 2D layers and 3D crystals ([[luo-2016-protein-assembly-review]]).

Fusion is powerful precisely because it inherits well-characterized, stable natural interfaces. Its limitation is the same fact: the designer chooses among the symmetries nature already provides, and the conformational flexibility of the borrowed building blocks can divert the assembly. The KDPGal–FkpA cube is the clean illustration — native mass spectrometry found **12-subunit tetrahedral and 18-subunit prism assemblies alongside the intended 24-subunit cube** ([[luo-2016-protein-assembly-review]]).

**Parameterize the backbone.** The second route generates the symmetric backbone from a closed-form geometric description. Its conceptual prerequisite is the demonstration that oligomeric state is *encodable*: [[harbury-1993-coiled-coil-oligomer-switch]] systematically mutated the hydrophobic **a** and **d** core positions of the dimeric GCN4-p1 leucine zipper in concert and obtained peptides forming **two-, three- and four-stranded** structures, showing that the geometry of buried apolar side chains — not merely the hydrophobic/polar pattern — selects the number of chains.

Two decades later this became a generative procedure. [[huang-2014-parametric-helical-bundles]] built backbones directly from the **Crick coiled-coil equations**, sampling the supercoil twist ω0, helical twist ω1, supercoil radius R0, per-helix phases and axial offsets on a grid, running Rosetta sequence design at each grid point, and filtering with Rosetta *ab initio* prediction before synthesis. The resulting designs — including a **pentameric parallel left-handed five-helix bundle** — were extremely stable (**extrapolated ΔG_fold > 60 kcal/mol**) with crystal structures showing nearly identical core packing to the models. The crucial mechanical detail is in the supplement: helices are arrayed symmetrically about the *z* axis, so for an *n*-helix bundle the superhelical phase takes values {0, 2π/n, … 2(n−1)π/n} ([[huang-2014-parametric-helical-bundles-supp]]). **Cyclic symmetry is exact by construction** — a property of the coordinate generator, never optimized for and never checked afterwards.

Published alongside it, [[thomson-2014-alpha-helical-barrels]] applied a related geometric logic to the sparse region above four helices. Classical coiled coils of two to four helices account for **more than 98% of known coiled-coil structures**; α-helical barrels, in which each helix contacts two neighbours through *two* independent hydrophobic seams, are the remainder, and most of their possible structures had never been observed. Enumerating the ways two seams can be accommodated in a heptad repeat, scoring with a knowledge-based function and building atomistic models produced discrete water-soluble **C5, C6 and C7** assemblies whose crystal structures matched the models — CC-Pent being the first crystal structure of a designed pentameric barrel and CC-Hept the first heptamer. That paper also introduced a theme worth carrying forward: the **channels are chemically defined and their diameters track oligomer state**, so a ring's lumen is itself a designable feature.

**Dock and design.** The third route specifies a protomer in advance, rigid-body docks copies of it into a higher-order symmetric arrangement, then optimizes the interface to make the assembled state low-energy. This was the mainstream approach, and [[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]] characterizes the state of the art it inherited in exactly these terms: current approaches required the structure of the monomer to be specified in advance and, **with the exception of parametrically designed helical bundles**, involved rigid-body docking of previously characterized monomers followed by interface optimization.

That single sentence names the binding constraint of the whole era. Because the protomer had to be specified first, the accessible space of oligomers was bounded by the space of already-solved monomers — which in particular excluded assemblies with **more intertwined chains**, where protomer and interface cannot be separated ([[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]]).

## 3. Specificity as a second axis

Getting the geometry right is not the same as getting the *addressing* right. In DNA, specificity comes from modular hydrogen bonds in the core of the double helix; in proteins it comes largely from buried hydrophobic packing complemented by irregular peripheral polar interactions ([[boyken-2016-hydrogen-bond-network-oligomers]]). A set of designed coiled-coil oligomers with hydrophobic cores may each fold correctly and still cross-assemble promiscuously.

[[boyken-2016-hydrogen-bond-network-oligomers]] added the missing axis with **HBNet**. Buried polar interactions are hard to design for two compounding reasons: the partially covalent hydrogen bond restricts donors and acceptors to narrow ranges of orientation and distance, and nearly all buried polar atoms must find a partner or incur a considerable desolvation penalty. Conventional design algorithms make this worse by expressing system energy as a sum of *pairwise* residue interactions, whereas a hydrogen-bond network is inherently many-body. HBNet instead precomputes hydrogen bonds and traverses the resulting graph to enumerate all networks of **three or more residues**, rejecting any network leaving a buried donor or acceptor unsatisfied. Hosting those networks in parametrically generatable coiled coils — which have repeating geometric cross sections, so a network can be reused at equivalent positions around the ring — produced **dimers, trimers and tetramers** built as two concentric rings of helices, in triangular, square and supercoiled topologies not previously seen. **Ten crystal structures** confirmed the designs, with left-handed trimers matching at sub-ångström RMSD and dimer networks as close as **0.39 Å**, and the networks conferred **interaction specificity in vivo**.

This is the point at which symmetric-oligomer design stops being "can we build a ring" and becomes "can we build a set of rings that do not talk to each other" — the prerequisite for anything multi-component.

## 4. The ceiling of the hand-built approach

[[courbet-2022-axle-rotor-assemblies]] is the best available measure of how far the parametric-plus-Rosetta toolchain could be pushed, and it is instructive that the paper is not really about making symmetric oligomers at all — it is about making symmetric oligomers *move*.

The premise, as [[courbet-2022-axle-rotor-assemblies]] states it, is that designed nanomaterials to that point — the paper names icosahedral nanocages and two-dimensional arrays — are assemblies whose components have **fixed orientations** relative to one another; the goal was a kinematic pair with internal degrees of freedom. The design proceeds hierarchically: rigid components first, directed self-assembly into hetero-oligomeric complexes second, shaping of the multistate energy landscape along the mechanical degrees of freedom third. The component library is substantial — **C3, C4, C5 and C8 cyclic rotors** and **D2, D3, D4, D5 and D8 dihedral axles** — confirmed by cryo-EM at 6.0 Å (C3 rotor, 28 Å internal diameter), 7.9 Å (C4 rotor, 57 Å internal diameter), 4.2 Å (D3 axle) and 7.4 Å (D8 axle) ([[courbet-2022-axle-rotor-assemblies]]). The payoff is the **symmetry-mismatched** assemblies: **D3–C3** and **D3–C5** axle-rotor systems that assemble in vitro and in vivo and, by cryo-EM, populate conformationally variable relative orientations reflecting the symmetry of the coupled components and the designed interface energy landscape — the D3–C3 system largely constrained, the mismatched D3–C5 rotor free to rotate and translate along the main symmetry axis ([[courbet-2022-axle-rotor-assemblies]]).

The result is a genuine conceptual advance: **symmetry as a mechanical variable**, where matching symmetries rigidify and mismatching them leaves a shallow periodic landscape along a rotational coordinate. But the supplement shows what it cost. Reaching a D3 axle required sampling **two different starting superhelical radii per helix (6 Å and 12 Å)** over a ~5 Å range in 0.5 Å bins, with helical phase swept 0°–90° in 10° steps, *z*-offsets from −1.51 Å to +1.51 Å in 0.1 Å steps, and supercoil phases fixed by hand at 0° for D3 and 30° for D2 — followed by HBNet interface design, RosettaRemodel helical fusion to cyclic partners, a second design round across the fusion, and SymDofMover for the D8 case ([[courbet-2022-axle-rotor-assemblies-supp]]). Every symmetry group is a separate engineering project.

## 5. Hallucination — symmetry as an objective (and, first, as an accident)

### 5.1 The method, and the oligomers nobody asked for

Hallucination as a design method was established by [[anishchenko-2021-hallucination]], and the mechanism is worth stating in its original, symmetry-free form, because everything the symmetric version does is a modification of this. Start from a **random amino acid sequence**, whose predicted inter-residue distance and orientation maps are diffuse and featureless. Run **Monte Carlo simulated annealing in sequence space**, at each step substituting a random residue at a random position, re-predicting with **trRosetta**, and accepting by the Metropolis criterion on the change in **Kullback–Leibler divergence** between the predicted distributions and generic length- and separation-dependent background distributions. The objective is pure **contrast** — make the network's prediction sharp — with no target structure specified anywhere; the authors' stated analogy is DeepDream, inverting a network trained to recognize patterns so that it generates them ([[anishchenko-2021-hallucination]]).

It worked, and the numbers set the baseline against which the symmetric variants should be read. After around **20,000 Monte Carlo steps** the maps were as featured as those for natural sequences; **2,000** proteins were generated; outputs were novel (median BLAST E-value **0.17** to UniRef90, average TM-score **0.78** to the closest PDB match) and spanned all-α, all-β and mixed α/β with **95 sub-folds**. Of **129** designs expressed, **27** gave SEC peaks for monomeric or small oligomeric species with CD spectra consistent with the targets, **21** were thermostable above 70 °C, and three structures were solved — NMR at **1.82 Å** backbone RMSD (design 0515), X-ray at **2.53 Å** (0217) and X-ray at **3.68 Å** overall but **1.32 Å** and **2.17 Å** across the N- and C-terminal halves (0738_mod) ([[anishchenko-2021-hallucination]]). A separate, telling detail: the closest PDB structure to 0738_mod is **Top7**, the canonical Rosetta de novo design ([[anishchenko-2021-hallucination-supp]]).

**And here is why this paper belongs in a chapter about symmetric oligomers.** Every sequence was modelled as a **monomer**, and yet **many of the hallucinated proteins formed oligomers in solution**. Design 0217 crystallized as a **dimer**, corroborated by SEC-MALS and NMR rotational correlation time; design 0417 self-associated transiently, which ultimately prevented NMR structure determination ([[anishchenko-2021-hallucination]]). The diagnosis is specific and, for this chapter, important: **trRosetta was trained on native structures including many homo- and hetero-oligomers, so it never learned to disfavour surface hydrophobic residues to the degree required for soluble monomers** — the network had absorbed the sequence signatures of native protein–protein interfaces, and β-sandwich designs suffered worst, plausibly reflecting immunoglobulin-fold interdomain contacts in the training data ([[anishchenko-2021-hallucination]]). The rescue was manual and effective: substituting exposed hydrophobics with polar residues converted several oligomers to monomers, demonstrated directly by paired before/after SEC traces ([[anishchenko-2021-hallucination-supp]]).

So the first thing a predictor-inverted generator did, unprompted, was **make homo-oligomers** — because interface-forming sequence patterns are what it had learned from the PDB. The wiki's reading is that this reframes what [[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]] achieved a year later. The hard part was never eliciting an oligomer from the network; it was **specifying which one**, converting an uncontrolled propensity into an addressable input.

### 5.2 Wicky — making valency an input

[[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]] removes the constraint that the protomer be known in advance, and it does so by changing what kind of object symmetry is. Instead of building symmetry into the coordinates, it **rewards** symmetry in a loss function — adding a symmetry term to the confidence objective of [[anishchenko-2021-hallucination]] and swapping trRosetta for AlphaFold2.

The method takes only two inputs: a chain length *L* and an oligomer valency *N*. Starting from a random sequence, a Monte Carlo search in sequence space feeds *N* copies of the current sequence into **AlphaFold2** ([[jumper-2021-alphafold2]]) and combines the network's confidence metrics — pLDDT and pTM — with a measure of cyclic symmetry, namely the **standard deviation of the distances between the centres of mass of adjacent protomers** in the predicted structure ([[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]]). Trajectories for monomers through heptamers with 65–130 residue chains typically coalesce within a few hundred steps, at a cost of roughly **1 to 7 CPU-days** per design ([[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]]). The outputs — called HALs — span all-α, mixed α/β and all-β topologies and differ from the cyclic de novo designs already in the PDB; median TM-scores to the closest PDB relative are **0.67 for protomers and 0.57 for oligomers**, with **29% of structures below the 0.5 fold-assignment cutoff** in CATH/SCOP, indicating substantial generalization beyond the training set ([[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]]).

**The failure that makes this paper worth reading closely.** 150 designs with pLDDT > 0.7 and pTM > 0.7 were selected for experimental testing, and virtually none showed appreciable soluble expression in *E. coli* — median soluble yield **9 mg/L** — with none of the marginally soluble ones showing both the expected SEC oligomerization state and a consistent circular-dichroism profile ([[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]]). The authors' diagnosis is **overfitting to the oracle**: hundreds of MCMC steps against AF2 generate adversarial sequences that the network confidently predicts as structured but which have aberrant biophysical properties, analogous to activation maximization producing unrealistic outputs in image classifiers ([[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]]).

This is not a new pathology so much as a much more severe instance of a known one. [[anishchenko-2021-hallucination]] had already reported that hallucinated sequences carry **exposed surface hydrophobics** inherited from the network's training on native oligomeric structures, and had fixed it by hand ([[anishchenko-2021-hallucination-supp]]). The wiki's reading is that adding a symmetry term makes the same failure much worse rather than introducing a different one: a cyclic-symmetry objective *rewards* inter-protomer contact, so the optimizer is now being paid to produce exactly the sticky surfaces that hallucination was already prone to. What changed between the two papers is not the diagnosis but the remedy — manual polar substitution on a handful of designs in 2021 ([[anishchenko-2021-hallucination-supp]]), versus wholesale sequence replacement by a dedicated inverse-folding network in 2022 ([[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]]).

The fix is instructive. Discard the hallucinated *sequences*, keep the hallucinated *backbones*, and redesign the sequences with [[dauparas-2022-proteinmpnn]] — 24 to 48 sequences per backbone, validated with AF2. Because dozens rather than hundreds of evaluations are performed, overfitting is far less likely; independently, an updated RoseTTAFold confidently predicted almost all ProteinMPNN sequences while failing on most of the original hallucinated ones. The redesigned set behaved completely differently: of 96 tested, **71 (74%) expressed at high levels** (median yield **247 mg/L**, a ~27-fold increase), **50 (52%) had SEC retention volumes consistent with the target oligomer** (30 of those monodisperse), **at least 21 (22%) had the correct oligomeric state by SEC-MALS**, and **67 of 71 (94%)** had circular-dichroism secondary-structure content consistent with the design. **54 of 71** retained secondary structure to 95 °C.

Structural validation was strong where it was attempted. Crystallization was tried for 19 designs and succeeded for **seven** (three C2, two C3, two C4), all with the correct oligomerization state and a **median Cα RMSD of 0.6 Å** at 1.8–3.4 Å resolution, with side-chain rotamers also matching ([[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]]). Six large designs with C6–C42 internal repeat symmetry gave negative-stain 3D reconstructions, and three were solved by cryo-EM at 4.38, 6.51 and 6.32 Å: a C15 homopentamer, a C18 homohexamer and a **C33 homotrimer**, **87, 99 and 100 Å in diameter** and 40–50 Å high, each with a continuous parallel β sheet lining the lumen and outer helices enforcing ring closure, with backbone RMSDs to the design models of **0.81, 1.69 and 2.30 Å** and TM-scores of 0.41–0.49 to anything in the PDB ([[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]]). For scale: the highest cyclic symmetry recorded in the PDB for a naturally occurring protein is **C39**, the vault proteins ([[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]]). Aggregated over the whole campaign, **27 of 30** designs evaluated by SEC-MALS, nsEM, cryo-EM or crystallography had the intended oligomeric state ([[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]]).

**What it could not do.** Hallucination-based cyclic design **fails for higher-order dihedral, tetrahedral, octahedral and icosahedral symmetries**, probably in part because such structures are far less represented in the PDB and therefore in the oracle's learned prior ([[watson-2023-rfdiffusion]]). The symmetry objective — a standard deviation over *adjacent* protomer centre-of-mass distances — is intrinsically a statement about a ring. It has no natural generalization to a point group with multiple distinct rotation axes.

## 6. RFdiffusion — symmetry as a constraint on sampling

[[watson-2023-rfdiffusion]] is the pivot of this chapter, and symmetric oligomer design is one of the capabilities it was built to unify — alongside unconditional and topology-constrained monomer design, motif and enzyme active-site scaffolding, and binder design. The model fine-tunes the RoseTTAFold structure-prediction network on a denoising task: starting from noised residue frames, it iteratively denoises to a coherent backbone, with conditioning inputs steering generation. For assemblies, the conditioning input is a **point-group symmetry operator**.

The mechanism is worth stating precisely, because it is the third distinct answer to "how do you make a design symmetric." Given a point group and a monomer chain length, random starting residue frames are generated for a **single** subunit as in the unconditional case, and *n*−1 copies are placed according to the point group. Because RFdiffusion inherits **equivariance** with respect to rotation and chain relabelling from RoseTTAFold, symmetry is *largely maintained* through the denoising predictions on its own; the model nonetheless **explicitly resymmetrizes at each step**, which changes the structures only slightly. For octahedral and icosahedral architectures only the smallest generating subset of monomers is modelled explicitly — for icosahedra, the subunits at the five-, three- and twofold axes — to reduce compute and memory. Design diversity is tuned by **scaling the noise added at inference**, and an **auxiliary inter- and intra-chain contact potential** guides symmetric sampling; external potentials are the same machinery used for ligand and metal pocket design ([[watson-2023-rfdiffusion-supp]]).

The striking result is that **RFdiffusion was not trained on symmetric inputs** and nonetheless achieves high in silico success rates on symmetric oligomers, particularly with the contact potential engaged ([[watson-2023-rfdiffusion]]). Symmetry here is neither built into a coordinate generator nor rewarded in a loss — it is **projected onto the state at every denoising step**, and the model's equivariance is what makes that projection nearly free.

**Coverage.** Cyclic designs included topologies not seen in the PDB — two-layer β barrels at **C10**, and mixed α/β topologies at **C8** whose closest TM-aligns in the PDB are **0.47 and 0.43** ([[watson-2023-rfdiffusion]]). Of **608 designs** selected for experimental characterization, **at least 87** had SEC oligomerization states within the 95% confidence interval of the calibration (**126 within the 99% interval**), and negative-stain EM of a **C3 design with 350-residue subunits (1,050 residues total)** matched the design both per-subunit and in overall architecture, including a distinctive pinwheel shape; C5 and C6 designs of more than 750 residues were also consistent with their models ([[watson-2023-rfdiffusion]]). A separate family of α/β barrel oligomers effectively extends the TIM barrel: **HE0626**, a C6 with 18 strands and 18 helices, and **HE0675**, a C8 with an inner ring of 16 strands and an outer ring of 16 helices in a 1:1 helix:strand pattern locally very similar to a TIM barrel, both confirmed by nsEM 3D reconstruction ([[watson-2023-rfdiffusion]]). The framing the authors give this is worth keeping: natural evolution extensively explored variations *within* the eight-strand TIM barrel, while a generative model more readily explores **global changes in barrel curvature** ([[watson-2023-rfdiffusion]]).

**Beyond cyclic.** This is where RFdiffusion separates from hallucination. SEC indicated that **38 D2, seven D3 and three D4** designs had the expected molecular weights (four, six and eight chains respectively); D3 and D4 2D class averages and 3D reconstructions matched the design topologies, and cryo-EM of the D4 design **HE0537** recapitulated the ~45° offset between tetrameric subunits ([[watson-2023-rfdiffusion]]). A **12-chain tetrahedron** (HE0964) gave consistent 2D class averages, and of **48 icosahedra** selected for validation, **HE0902** — a **15 nm**, highly porous assembly — formed homogeneous particles whose 2D class averages and 3D reconstruction closely match the design model, with triangular hubs arrayed around empty C5 axes and outward-facing N and C termini suited to antigen display ([[watson-2023-rfdiffusion]]).

Two things should be read together here. The **coverage** is unprecedented: C, D, T and I in one framework, from noise, with no per-symmetry engineering of the kind [[courbet-2022-axle-rotor-assemblies-supp]] documents. The **yield** is sobering: 87 of 608 by SEC, one validated icosahedron out of 48 attempts. Generality and success rate are different quantities, and this chapter's reading is that RFdiffusion's contribution to symmetric design is overwhelmingly the former.

## 7. Three ways to impose symmetry, compared

The three mechanisms are easy to conflate because they produce superficially similar objects. Stated side by side:

**Exact by construction.** In parametric design the symmetry is a property of the coordinate generator — the superhelical phases are *set* to {0, 2π/n, …} and no other value is representable ([[huang-2014-parametric-helical-bundles-supp]]). Symmetry error is identically zero. The price is expressivity: only folds the parameterization can write down, in practice helical bundles and barrels ([[huang-2014-parametric-helical-bundles]], [[thomson-2014-alpha-helical-barrels]]).

**Rewarded.** In hallucination the symmetry is a **term in a loss** — the standard deviation of adjacent-protomer centre-of-mass distances — added to the pure confidence objective that [[anishchenko-2021-hallucination]] established and optimized jointly with it ([[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]]). Expressivity is enormous: protomer and assembly are discovered together, so intertwined topologies inaccessible to dock-and-design become reachable, and the outputs generalize well past the training set. Three prices are paid. The optimization is expensive (1–7 CPU-days per design). Optimizing *against* a predictor for hundreds or thousands of steps invites adversarial solutions — the 150-design expression failure of [[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]], foreshadowed by the surface-hydrophobic problem already documented in [[anishchenko-2021-hallucination]]. And the objective is ring-shaped: a statement about *adjacent* protomers does not extend to point groups with several distinct axes, which is why the approach fails for dihedral and cubic symmetries ([[watson-2023-rfdiffusion]]).

**Projected.** In diffusion the symmetry is applied to the **state**, at initialization and again at every denoising step, and the model's equivariance means the denoiser's own predictions nearly respect it already ([[watson-2023-rfdiffusion]], [[watson-2023-rfdiffusion-supp]]). Symmetry error is driven to near zero without ever entering the objective. Expressivity extends to any point group, including cubic ones, and the asymmetric-unit trick keeps icosahedral design tractable. The price is that success is now dominated by whether the *interface* the model invents is physically real — hence the reliance on an auxiliary contact potential, and the 87-of-608 SEC yield.

The through-line the wiki draws from this: **as symmetry moved from the coordinates into the sampling process, the binding constraint moved from geometry to interface quality.** The parametric era's hard problem was writing down a backbone; the diffusion era's hard problem is that writing down backbones is easy and most of them do not assemble.

## 8. Sequence design is not a detail

It would be easy to treat sequence design as a downstream step. The symmetric-oligomer literature is the strongest evidence in this wiki that it is not.

[[dauparas-2022-proteinmpnn]] predicts sequence autoregressively from backbone geometry alone and explicitly handles cyclic oligomers, nanoparticles and interfaces, including **tied/symmetric positions**. The mechanism enabling tying is the **random-order autoregressive decoding** scheme — a detail stated in the main text but derived only in the supplement ([[dauparas-2022-proteinmpnn-supp]]) — and the same tied-decoding machinery underlies symmetric design in [[watson-2023-rfdiffusion-supp]]. Tying matters for a specific reason: under C*n* symmetry, the *n* copies of a residue position are the same residue by definition, so sequence design must either respect that constraint or be corrected afterwards.

The empirical case is the Wicky rescue described above. The same backbones, sequenced two different ways, gave a median soluble yield of **9 mg/L** versus **247 mg/L**, and went from zero validated designs to 27 of 30 with the intended oligomeric state ([[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]]). The generator was never the problem; the sequences were. That result also justifies the pipeline shape adopted everywhere afterward — **generate backbone → design sequence with ProteinMPNN → filter with AlphaFold2** ([[watson-2023-rfdiffusion]]) — and it is a large part of why [[dauparas-2022-proteinmpnn]] shows up as a dependency of essentially every later symmetric-design result.

## 9. What actually gets validated

Symmetric assemblies are unusually well served by orthogonal experimental methods, and the literature has settled into a fairly consistent ladder. It is worth being explicit about it, because "success rate" means different things at different rungs.

*In silico*, success is defined by AlphaFold2 self-consistency: **pAE < 5**, **pLDDT > 90** (relaxed to > 80 for motif cases) and backbone RMSD to the design under ~1.0–1.5 Å; these thresholds, not wet-lab data, define the success rates quoted throughout [[watson-2023-rfdiffusion]] ([[watson-2023-rfdiffusion-supp]]). Hallucination used a looser in-house bar — pLDDT > 0.75 and RMSD to the original backbone < 1.5 Å for the ProteinMPNN redesigns ([[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]]).

Experimentally the rungs are: **soluble expression**, then **SEC** retention volume against a calibration curve (cheap, high-throughput, and the metric behind both the 50/96 of [[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]] and the 87/608 of [[watson-2023-rfdiffusion]]), then **SEC-MALS** for an absolute mass rather than a hydrodynamic proxy, then **circular dichroism** for secondary-structure content and thermal stability, then **negative-stain EM** for shape and 2D class averages, then **cryo-EM** for a 3D reconstruction, and finally **crystallography** for atomic accuracy. The gap between the rungs is real: in [[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]], 52% passed SEC but only ~22% passed SEC-MALS on the same set.

Two observations follow. First, symmetric assemblies are the one design class where **EM is routinely decisive**, because the assemblies are large enough — the giant HAL rings exceeded 100 kDa, which is what made them well suited to EM characterization ([[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]]) — and because a ring's symmetry is directly legible in a 2D class average. Second, the crystallization rate itself carries information: seven of 19 attempted HALs formed diffracting crystals, which the authors note is considerably higher than is typical for Rosetta de novo designs ([[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]]).

## 10. After RFdiffusion

The symmetric-design capability has propagated rather than plateaued. [[ingraham-2023-chroma]], developed independently and contemporaneously at Generate Biomedicines, frames design as **Bayesian inference under external constraints**, with **symmetry** among the first-class conditioners alongside substructure, shape, semantic class and natural-language prompts; conditioners enter as likelihood or constraint terms on the diffusion posterior, giving one uniform mechanism for imposing symmetry ([[ingraham-2023-chroma-supp]]). That two lineages arrived at conditional diffusion with symmetry conditioning separately is, on this wiki's reading, evidence that the formulation is natural rather than incidental — the same argument made at the level of the whole field in [[overview]].

[[butcher-2025-rfdiffusion3]] carries the capability into the all-atom, token-plus-atom regime, with a **dedicated inference loop for symmetric design** — token initializers plus symmetry handling — distinct from the main inference loop, explicitly carrying forward the symmetry capability of [[watson-2023-rfdiffusion-supp]] ([[butcher-2025-rfdiffusion3-supp]]). Given that RFdiffusion3 matches or beats its predecessors at roughly **one-tenth the compute** ([[butcher-2025-rfdiffusion3]]), and that symmetric-oligomer success rates are dominated by yield rather than coverage, cheaper sampling is a directly relevant improvement: the 87-of-608 problem is partly a compute-budget problem.

One further thread connects symmetry to function. [[watson-2023-rfdiffusion]] scaffolds **symmetric functional motifs** — C3-symmetric trimers rigidly holding three binding domains, and C4-symmetric histidine square-planar metal sites — noting that the symmetry of an assembly can be matched to the symmetry of a coordination geometry or a viral glycoprotein. Symmetric oligomer design and [[motif-scaffolding]] are not separate problems at the level of the model; they are two conditioning inputs to the same generator.

## 11. Open questions

- **Why is the yield what it is?** No ingested source diagnoses *why* only 87 of 608 symmetric RFdiffusion designs pass SEC ([[watson-2023-rfdiffusion]]), or whether failures are dominated by interface quality, kinetic trapping in the wrong oligomeric state, or solubility. The chapter's two mechanistic failure accounts — surface hydrophobics inherited from training on native oligomers ([[anishchenko-2021-hallucination]]) and adversarial overfitting to the oracle ([[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]]) — both concern *hallucination*, and neither transfers cleanly to a diffusion model that never optimizes against a predictor. Whether diffusion's symmetric failures share a cause with hallucination's is open.
- **Did the interface prior survive the transition?** [[anishchenko-2021-hallucination]] attributes unintended oligomerization to the network having learned the sequence signatures of native protein–protein interfaces from the PDB. RFdiffusion is fine-tuned from a structure predictor trained on the same kind of data ([[watson-2023-rfdiffusion]]), so the same prior is presumably present — but whether it *helps* symmetric design (a useful bias toward realistic interfaces) or *hurts* it (a bias toward native-like rather than designed interfaces) is not addressed by any ingested source.
- **Does success rate decay with symmetry order, and how?** The numbers are suggestive — 38 D2 versus seven D3 versus three D4 ([[watson-2023-rfdiffusion]]) — but the denominators per symmetry group are not stated in the main text, so no rate can be computed. [[watson-2023-rfdiffusion-supp]] indicates per-symmetry SEC success rates are characterized in the SI; extracting them would settle this.
- **Is exact resymmetrization necessary?** [[watson-2023-rfdiffusion]] reports that explicit resymmetrization at each step changes structures only slightly, since equivariance largely preserves symmetry anyway. Whether the residual asymmetry is noise or a useful relaxation is not addressed.
- **Can hallucination's expressivity be recovered inside diffusion?** Hallucination discovered intertwined protomer/assembly topologies precisely because it never separated them ([[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]]). Whether symmetric diffusion samples that same region, or has quietly reintroduced a bias toward separable protomers, is untested in the ingested sources.
- **Two-component and heteromeric assemblies.** [[courbet-2022-axle-rotor-assemblies]] achieves hetero-oligomeric coupling by design, and the specificity machinery of [[boyken-2016-hydrogen-bond-network-oligomers]] exists to make orthogonal components possible, but the wiki has no ingested source on designed **two-component** symmetric nanomaterials — see the flagged references below.
- **Benchmarks.** There is no shared benchmark for symmetric-oligomer design comparable to the PPI or motif-scaffolding suites in [[butcher-2025-rfdiffusion3-supp]], so cross-paper numbers in this chapter are not strictly comparable. This is an instance of the `benchmarks/` gap already noted in [[overview]].

## 12. Flagged references — the history this chapter cannot yet cite

The sections above are written strictly from ingested sources, per the sourcing discipline in `CLAUDE.md`. Several load-bearing pieces of the historical record are therefore either cited **second-hand through a review** or absent entirely. Supplying these PDFs into `sources/` would let the chapter stand on primary literature. Listed in rough order of how much they would improve the chapter; these are also mirrored in [[index]].

**Currently cited only second-hand, through [[luo-2016-protein-assembly-review]]:**

1. **Padilla, Colovos & Yeates, *PNAS* 2001 — "Nanohedra: using symmetry to design self-assembling protein cages, layers, crystals and filaments."** [[luo-2016-protein-assembly-review]] credits this as the founding symmetric-fusion result (the M1-matrix/bromoperoxidase tetrahedral cage). Section 2 of this chapter rests on a review's account of it. This is the single most valuable addition.
2. **The Yeates-lab follow-ups on the crystallographically resolved 12-subunit cage** — the refinement that [[luo-2016-protein-assembly-review]] describes as producing the homogeneous 16-nm cage with a 5-nm central opening, again currently reachable only through the review.

**Named as prior art by ingested sources, but with no primary paper in `sources/`:**

3. **King et al., *Science* 2012 — "Computational design of self-assembling protein nanomaterials with atomic level accuracy."** Appears in the reference list of [[luo-2016-protein-assembly-review]]; the icosahedral-nanocage line it founded is what [[courbet-2022-axle-rotor-assemblies]] refers to as the fixed-orientation nanomaterials it sets out to move beyond. The chapter currently cannot describe how those cages were designed.
4. **King et al., *Nature* 2014 — "Accurate design of co-assembling multi-component protein nanomaterials."** The two-component branch; needed for the open question in §11.
5. **Bale et al., *Science* 2016 — "Accurate design of megadalton-scale two-component icosahedral protein complexes."** The scale demonstration; also the natural bridge to vaccine-platform applications, which [[watson-2023-rfdiffusion]] invokes without this wiki being able to support it.
