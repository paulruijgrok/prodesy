# Protein assembly — versatile approaches to construct highly ordered nanostructures

A 60-page *Chemical Reviews* survey of how ordered protein nanostructures are built. Ingested for the wiki mainly as a **historical reference for the pre-deep-learning symmetry literature**, which the rest of `sources/` does not otherwise cover.

**Scope.** Organizes the field into biotechnological strategies (symmetry-based assembly, computational design, peptide-mediated assembly, template-induced assembly, in vivo assembly), chemical strategies (receptor–ligand pairs, metal coordination, electrostatics, host–guest recognition, polymer conjugates, covalent self-assembly) and applications (biosensors, biocatalytic materials, diagnostics and therapy). Only §2.1 and §2.2 are directly load-bearing for [[symmetric-oligomer-design]].

**Key content used by this wiki.**

- **Why symmetry at all.** Symmetric structures are ubiquitous in biology because symmetric interfaces can be energetically favourable under evolutionary pressure; natural proteins oligomerize into linear, cyclic, dihedral, cubic, planar and spatial symmetries. Because the relative orientation of two adjacent domains and the complementarity of their contact interfaces determine the multiplicity of the complex, it becomes possible to design nanoscale assemblies **predictably**, by geometrically controlled fusion of two independent oligomeric domains into one larger molecule.
- **The symmetric-fusion origin (Yeates, 2001).** The review credits Yeates and co-workers with the pioneering **genetic oligomeric fusion** method: dimeric M1 matrix protein and trimeric bromoperoxidase were held in a predetermined orientation by fusion through a semirigid helical linker, self-assembling into a **12-subunit tetrahedral cage** when the geometric rule that the 3-fold and 2-fold rotational axes intersect at an appropriate non-orthogonal angle was satisfied. Replacing the trimeric partner with a dimerizing carboxylesterase instead produced **filaments**. A later refinement of the same design gave a homogeneous **12-subunit, 16-nm cage with a 5-nm central opening**, resolved crystallographically.
- **The design rules that follow.** Fusion partners must be stable and mutually recognizing under the same conditions; natural oligomers usually carry several symmetry operators, so only the axis satisfying the design requirement is usable; and linkers or small-molecule mediators give additional control over pre-existing interfaces. Extensions covered include a ball-and-spike gp5C–LisDps assembly, the WA20–foldon "nanohedra" 6-mer and 12-mer assemblies, a KDPGal–FkpA cube-shaped cage with **225 Å outer and 132 Å inner diameter** under octahedral symmetry, and **symmetry-matching fusion** producing 1D filaments, 2D layers and 3D crystals from D2/D4/C2 oligomers sharing an aligned symmetry axis.
- **Its own stated limits.** The review notes that symmetry-based fusion works well for relatively simple architectures but that constructing **large, high-level superstructures remains a considerable challenge**, because the geometric arrangement between symmetry axes must be highly accurate and conformational flexibility in the building blocks can divert assembly — the KDPGal–FkpA construct was found by native mass spectrometry to populate **12-subunit tetrahedral and 18-subunit prism assemblies alongside the intended 24-subunit cube**.
- **Where computation enters.** Computational design is presented as the complement to fusion: unlike fusion, it can create **entirely new interfaces** between naturally occurring proteins and therefore access a far more diverse range of shapes, with accuracy limited by the energy function and conformational sampling.

**Significance / how the wiki uses it.** This is the wiki's only ingested source for the **symmetry-by-fusion** lineage and for the pre-2016 nanostructure literature generally. It supplies the historical frame that [[symmetric-oligomer-design]] opens with. Two cautions apply: it is a **review**, so claims sourced here are second-hand, and its coverage of the Baker-lab computational nanomaterial line (self-assembling nanomaterials with atomic-level accuracy, two-component cages, icosahedral assemblies) is cursory — those primary papers are listed as flagged references in [[index]] rather than being asserted from this review.
