# Protein Hunter — hallucination inside a diffusion-based structure predictor

A **fine-tuning-free** design framework: rather than training a generator or back-propagating through a predictor, it exploits the observation that diffusion-based structure-prediction models will **hallucinate plausible structures from an uninformative sequence**, and then improves them by alternating sequence redesign with structure re-prediction.

**Problem.** Designing protein–biomolecule interactions de novo requires co-optimizing sequence and structure, which is difficult and computationally expensive. The paper situates itself against two existing families: generative diffusion ([[watson-2023-rfdiffusion]], [[butcher-2025-rfdiffusion3]]) which can create novel folds, assemblies and binders; and gradient-based co-refinement — **BindCraft** (Pacesa et al. 2025) and **BoltzDesign** (Cho et al. 2025) — whose reliance on gradient descent leads to slow convergence.

**Approach.** Start from an **all-X sequence** (every position the unknown-residue token) and predict its structure with an AF3-style model. Then cycle: redesign the sequence with ProteinMPNN (or LigandMPNN for ligand tasks), re-predict the complex, repeat. There is **no diffusion over coordinates and no fine-tuning** — the generative capacity is borrowed wholesale from a prediction model's willingness to hallucinate.

**Why the X token.** Among all initialization tokens, X-initiated designs achieved the highest ipTM scores and the lowest loop fractions, i.e. the most compact and stable folds. AF3-style models encode unknown PDB residues as X, so the token carries a learned "something structured belongs here" prior. A consequence the paper notes: because X shares alanine's backbone atoms (N, Cα, C, Cβ), initial backbones from X are often redesigned by ProteinMPNN with **high alanine content**, which repeated cycles then refine away.

**Why Boltz-2 wins among predictors.** Boltz-2 achieved the highest *in silico* success rate of the diffusion-based predictors tested (vs. Chai-1 and AF3), and the paper offers a mechanistic hypothesis: Boltz-2 encodes each non-canonical residue as a **single learned token**, so X acts as one residue, whereas AF3 and Chai-1 use atom-level tokenization, increasing sampling complexity; and AF3 was fine-tuned on distilled AF2 predictions, which encourages spaghetti-like structures in low-confidence regions, suppressing exactly the hallucination this method depends on.

## Key results

- **Unconditional generation** at 100–900 residues with 20 sequences per length, assessed by backbone RMSD pre- vs post-refolding and pLDDT: confident, designable structures with high TM-score agreement between cycle-5 structures and AF3 re-predictions, demonstrated up to 500–600 residues.
- **Protein binder design.** Against 11 targets, Protein Hunter with Boltz-2 achieved **higher ipTM than RFdiffusion on 9 of 11 targets**, and with Chai-1 on **7 of 11**, while maintaining strong pLDDT. Multimeric targets work too: binders were generated on the TNF-α trimer surface without disrupting the trimer.
- **Small-molecule binders.** On four chemically diverse targets previously tested with RFdiffusionAA and BoltzDesign1, with success defined as **backbone RMSD ≤ 1.5 Å, ligand RMSD ≤ 5 Å, interface PAE ≤ 1.5 and ipTM ≥ 0.8** (evaluated by AlphaFold3, 40 designs per ligand), Protein Hunter reached **higher success rates on all targets** than RFdiffusionAA, BoltzDesign2, and its own single-pass mode.
- **Cyclic peptides.** MDM2-binding cyclic peptide designs improve in AF3 interface pAE(min) and ipTM from cycle 0 to cycle 4, at 14, 16 and 18 residues.
- **Scope.** Also demonstrated: multi-motif scaffolding (a heme-binding protein with two motifs within 5 Å all-atom distance of the ligand held fixed), partial redesign, antibody CDR redesign, and DNA/RNA binders including a mutant TERT promoter target.

**The efficiency claim.** Iterative cycling "reduces the need for extensive sequence sampling": structure and sequence are progressively co-optimized across cycles, yielding strong backbone–sequence compatibility without large MPNN sampling budgets. This is the paper's answer to the compute problem that [[didi-2026-complexa-scaling]] attacks with search inside a trained prior.

**A limitation the paper states against itself.** Structures generated by AF3-style models exhibited **lower structural diversity than RFdiffusion's**, reflecting a bias toward α-helical topologies. The method inherits the predictor's prior, including its preferences. (The paper also notes RFdiffusion was evaluated with single-sequence refolding for comparability, which may understate its success rate — a comparability caveat of exactly the kind catalogued in [[ruijgrok-2026-binder-pipeline-comparison]].)

## Significance / relation to the field

Completes the hallucination lineage. [[anishchenko-2021-hallucination]] inverted a predictor by MCMC with no prior; [[wicky-2022-symmetric-assembly-design]] added an objective and discovered adversarial overfitting; [[frank-2024-relaxed-sequence-optimization]] replaced MCMC with gradients; [[didi-2026-complexa-scaling]] put the search inside a purpose-trained generative prior. Protein Hunter takes the opposite route to the same end: it leaves the predictor untouched and **lets the predictor be the prior**, using a greedy predict-then-redesign cycle rather than gradients or diffusion. That it works at all is evidence that what hallucination needed was a well-behaved prior, not a better optimizer — which is the same conclusion [[didi-2026-complexa-scaling]] reaches by building one.

**Relation to this project.** ProteinHunter is one of the four locally operated generative pipelines in [[ruijgrok-2026-binder-pipeline-comparison]], and the operational record adds detail the paper does not. It is the only pipeline in the project that supplies **no target structure at all** — target as sequence plus MSA, conformation re-predicted every cycle, `--template_path` present but never passed — recorded there as "the sharpest representational difference in the project." Its hotspot enforcement is the project's strictest (a Boltz pocket constraint *plus* an explicit post-hoc heavy-atom contact filter at cycle 0 and again at a promotion gate). And its measured cost is 510.2 GPU-hours over 1,255 tasks at 15.40 GPU-min per design. Critically for any rate quoted from it: the **promotion gate sets the denominator before scoring**, losing 79% of tasks, and moving the gate from 0.8 to 0.7 shifted promotion from 14.1% to 43.8% on byte-matched generation — so the published ipTM-based comparisons and the project's rates are not the same kind of number.
